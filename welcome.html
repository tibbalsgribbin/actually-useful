Welcome to Actually Useful.

The extension is installed. Here's what it does — and how to get the most out of it.

What you're looking at

Open any Amazon search and the Actually Useful panel appears on the right. Here's what each part does.

  • Sort

    Sort section

    Sort by best value (price per unit), lowest price, fastest delivery, or fastest free delivery. Load up to 7 pages of results right here — more results means more to work with.

  • Move ads

    Move ads to end of results

    Sponsored listings don't disappear — they move to the bottom. You can still see them, but they're out of the way.

  • Pages

    Pages slider + Re-sync

    Drag the slider to load more pages. Hit Re-sync after changing Amazon's own filters — it reloads everything without losing your work.

  • Filters

    Filters section

    Minimum reviews, minimum rating, price range, keywords, sources, Prime only, SNAP EBT, FSA/HSA, Climate Pledge Friendly, Small Business. Use one or combine several.

  • Brands

    Brand filters

    Move Amazon house brands to the end of results. Move unrecognized or pseudo-brands to the end. Neither hides items — they're still there if you want them.

  • Delivery

    Hide slow shipping

    Hide anything that won't arrive within your chosen window. A good proxy for overseas shipping — if it takes three weeks, it's probably not coming from a local warehouse.

  • Results

    Result cards

    Each card shows the real price per unit, any coupons or Subscribe & Save discount, both delivery dates, brand, and rating. The ★ marks the best value item across all results.

  • Compare bar

    Check items + Compare

    Check any item to add it to your shortlist. The compare bar at the bottom shows how many you've selected. Hit Compare when you're ready — everything opens side by side on the compare page.

  • Brand rules

    Per-card brand controls

    Each card with a detected brand shows "Always show / Always hide." Use these to teach the extension your preferences across all searches.

  • Footer

    Results summary + feedback

    The footer tells you how many results matched, how many pages loaded, and how many were filtered out. "Give feedback" goes to the feedback form.

The workflow

Actually Useful is built around a specific sequence. It works best when you follow it.

1

Start with Amazon's filters

Amazon's category filters are genuinely good. Use them first — department, brand, size, material, whatever narrows the category. Actually Useful picks up from there.

2

Load more pages

Drag the Pages slider. More results means more to compare — and the best value item is often buried on page 4. Load all 7 if you have time. Load 3 if you're in a hurry.

Tip: load pages first, then apply filters. Filters run on whatever's already loaded.

3

Sort and filter

Sort by Best value to see real price-per-unit at the top. Then use filters to narrow: keyword, Prime, delivery window, rating. The results summary in the footer tells you exactly what got filtered out.

Tip: collapse sections you've set — the panel gets shorter and you can see your results better.

4

Shortlist as you scroll

Check any item to add it to your shortlist. You don't have to be selective here — check everything that looks plausible. You'll narrow down on the compare page. Add a note to any item if something catches your eye.

5

Hit Compare

Click the Compare button in the bar at the bottom of the panel. Everything you checked opens in a clean side-by-side table — price, unit price, delivery, coupons, ratings, notes, all of it. Sort any column. Filter further. Share with someone.

Tip: the compare page has its own filter bar. Use it to do a second pass — hide slow shipping, filter to Prime, sort by unit price — without going back to the search.

Features in depth

A few things worth knowing about how specific features work.

Price per unit

Actually Useful calculates $/oz, $/count, $/sheet, $/load — whatever makes sense for the product. When Amazon's number is wrong, it recalculates from the title. A ★ marks the best value item across all visible results.

The ppuNote on each card explains how the unit was determined.

Keyword filter

Type a word to show only results containing it. Add a minus sign in front to exclude it. Combine them. The filter runs on product titles — it's exact text matching, not fuzzy search.

Example: "pods -scent" shows pods and hides anything with "scent" in the title.

Delivery sorting

Sort by fastest delivery (any type) or fastest free delivery. Actually Useful captures both free and paid delivery windows and the cutoff time for next-day orders.

Items with no delivery date are sorted to the end, not treated as fast.

Coupons and Subscribe & Save

If a product has a coupon or S&S discount, it shows on the card. The unit price calculation doesn't factor these in by default — the savings are shown separately so you can decide.

Brand filter

The extension uses a combination of heuristics and a bundled blocklist to flag pseudo-brands and dropship listings. "Move unrecognized brands to end" is off by default — turn it on for categories like clothing where junk brands are common.

Use "Always show" on a brand card to prevent it from being flagged in the future.

Re-sync

Changed Amazon's own filters (department, brand, size) while Actually Useful was open? Hit Re-sync. It reloads results for the new filter state without clearing your shortlist or panel settings.

SNAP EBT and FSA/HSA

Items eligible for SNAP EBT or FSA/HSA spending show a badge on the card. You can filter to show only eligible items using the Filters section.

Sources

Amazon search results sometimes include Fresh (grocery) and other sources. The source filter lets you show or hide specific sources. Useful if you only want standard Amazon results, or only Fresh.
